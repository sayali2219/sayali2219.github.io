01 / WARM MINIMAL

Quiet, warm and easy to live with.

Layer cream, timber, woven textures and soft curves. Keep the palette restrained and let texture do the work.

02 / EARTH + WOOD

Natural colour with enough depth.

Muted green, oak and stone create a kitchen that feels warm without becoming visually heavy.

03 / SOFT LAYERING

A bedroom that feels restful.

Use tonal fabrics, warm timber and low, soft lighting to make the room feel quieter at night.

Living

Start with circulation.

Leave enough space to move between seating, balcony doors and storage before adding decor.

Kitchen

Plan the work triangle.

Think through preparation, cooking, washing and storage together instead of selecting finishes first.

Bedroom

Make rest feel easy.

Keep lighting layered, storage accessible and the palette calm enough to unwind.

Bathroom

Choose durability early.

Plan finishes and storage around moisture, cleaning and the way the room is actually used.

01 / TIMBER + STONE

Warm structure, quiet contrast.

Natural timber brings warmth while stone adds weight and durability. Keep the surrounding palette restrained.

02 / METAL + WOOD

A little contrast goes a long way.

Use black or warm metal selectively through handles, lights and furniture so the room does not feel flat.

03 / TEXTURE + LIGHT

Make the room feel layered.

Linen, cane, plaster, rugs and soft lighting can add depth without adding visual clutter.

2026 / INDIAN HOMES

Warm, tactile, personal.

Current 2026 design coverage points toward warmer palettes, natural materials, softer silhouettes and spaces that feel collected rather than staged. Kitchens are increasingly treated as lived-in rooms, with freestanding elements, layered lighting and more expressive stone and finishes.

The takeaway isn't to copy a trend. Choose what will still make sense in your home five years from now.

2BHK / 3BHK

Plan the home before you decorate it.

Start with movement and storage before colours and decor. Check circulation around beds and sofas, kitchen work zones, wardrobe depth, electrical points, natural light and where everyday clutter will actually go.

A beautiful moodboard cannot rescue a badly planned room.

DESIGN-ONLY VS TURNKEY

Which model suits you?

Design-only: you hire a designer for the thinking, planning and design package, then choose who executes it. Turnkey: one provider typically coordinates design, procurement, vendors and execution. Neither is automatically better. The right choice depends on how much control and involvement you want.

Artisan Mate is built for homeowners who want the first model.

FAQs

Questions homeowners usually ask.

Do you execute the interior work?

No. Artisan Mate focuses on interior design and consultation. You can execute through a contractor, carpenter, modular company or other vendors of your choice.

Can I work with you if I live outside a metro city?

Yes. The service is designed to work remotely, so homeowners across India can discuss a project and share plans, measurements, photos and videos online.

Can you work with an existing contractor?

Yes. That is one of the reasons the design-only approach exists: you keep your preferred execution team while the design is developed separately.

Do you only design new homes?

No. The approach can work for new apartments, independent homes, renovations and spaces that simply need better planning or a fresh design direction.

Will I receive drawings and a design package?

The exact deliverables depend on the scope you choose. We confirm what is included before the project begins.
